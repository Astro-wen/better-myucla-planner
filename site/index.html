Better MyUCLA

Drag classes around your Class Planner, instead of clicking the arrow one position at a time.

Not made by, endorsed by, or affiliated with UCLA

What the buttons do

  • MyUCLA's own arrows. Still there, still one position per click.
  • Drag. Hold this and move the class anywhere. Drag toward the top or bottom of the window and the page scrolls with you.
  • Send to the top. One click, from anywhere in the list.
  • Jump to a position. Pick a number and it goes there.
  • Note. Anything you want to remember. Stays on your computer.
  • Collapse. Hide the section table for this class.

Nothing reaches MyUCLA until you press Save

Rearrange as much as you like first. A bar at the bottom of the window keeps count, and Save then clicks MyUCLA's own arrows for you.

Install it

About two minutes, in Chrome or anything built on Chrome.

Download the latest version
  1. Unzip it somewhere permanent. Documents, not Downloads.
  2. Open chrome://extensions in a new tab.
  3. Turn on Developer mode, top right.
  4. Click Load unpacked, top left.
  5. Choose the dist folder inside the folder you unzipped.
  6. Open your Class Planner and scroll to Class Plan.

Two things to expect.

Chrome warns about developer-mode extensions every time it starts. That is its standard notice for anything not installed from its store. Dismiss it.

There is no auto-update. For a new version, unzip it over the same folder, then press reload on the card in chrome://extensions. A different folder loses your notes and settings.

Turning it off

Click the extension icon in the toolbar. The switch at the top puts the Class Planner back exactly as MyUCLA made it, immediately.

The other switch is optional: MyUCLA counts only clicks as "still here", so reading your plan for fifteen minutes signs you out. Turn it on and scrolling counts too.

What it can and cannot reach

  • One page only: be.my.ucla.edu/ClassPlanner/ClassPlan.aspx.
  • One permission: local storage, for your notes and collapsed classes.
  • No server, no account. Nothing leaves your browser.
  • Never enrolls, drops, waitlists, or watches for open seats. Never reads your password, cookies, UID, grades, or DARS.
  • Source is public: on GitHub.

Something broke?

MyUCLA can change its pages at any time. When it does, this stops rather than guesses, so the worst case should be that it does nothing. Tell me here. Please blur anything identifying in screenshots.
